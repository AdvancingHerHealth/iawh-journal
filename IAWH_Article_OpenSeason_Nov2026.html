It’s Open Season. Don’t Let Your Plan Renew on Autopilot.

Coverage Check, Part 1  |  Medicare & Marketplace  |  November 2026

Compare your plan every year, even if you’re happy with it.

Two major health insurance enrollment windows are open right now. If you or someone you love has Medicare or buys coverage on the Marketplace, now is the time to look at next year’s costs.

Plans and prices change every year, and 2027 brings bigger changes than usual.

Why this matters for women: More than half of the people on Medicare are women, and women make up nearly two-thirds of beneficiaries 85 and older. Women also live on less: their median income is about $5,000 a year lower than men’s, and lower still for Black and Hispanic women. And women are more likely to manage several chronic conditions at once. That makes choosing the right plan, with the right drugs and doctors, especially important. If you’re helping a mother, aunt or grandmother compare plans this season, you’re not alone.

Medicare: Open Enrollment ends Sunday, December 7

Medicare’s Open Enrollment runs October 15 through December 7. Any changes you make take effect January 1. What’s new for 2027:

  • The yearly cap on out-of-pocket costs for prescription drugs rises to $2,400, up from $2,100.
  • The standard deductible for drug plans rises to $700, up from $615.
  • Medicare-negotiated prices take effect for 15 drugs, including Ozempic, Rybelsus and Wegovy.
  • The monthly Part B premium is projected to rise to about $209.50. The official amount will be announced later this fall.

Even if you’re happy with your plan, check it. Drug lists, provider networks and costs can change from year to year.

Marketplace coverage: Open Enrollment runs through January

If you buy your own insurance through HealthCare.gov or your state’s marketplace, Open Enrollment began November 1. Premiums are rising again in 2027. The extra federal help that lowered premiums for most enrollees expired and was not renewed. People earning more than 400% of the federal poverty level no longer get any help with premiums, and some insurers are leaving the market.

Log in, compare plans and update your income. Don’t let your plan renew automatically without checking the new price.

Women & Coverage: What to Know This Open Season

By the numbers

  • About 35 million women have Medicare, more than half of all beneficiaries.
  • Women are 63% of beneficiaries 85 and older.
  • Women’s median income on Medicare: $33,750, vs. $38,950 for men. Black women: $25,500. Hispanic women: $18,750.
  • Women have about $30,000 less in median savings than men.
  • 29% of women on Medicare have osteoporosis (vs. 7% of men); 31% have depression (vs. 21%).

2027 drug changes to watch

  • Ibrance (breast cancer) gets a lower Medicare-negotiated price starting Jan. 1.
  • So do four diabetes drugs: Ozempic, Rybelsus, Tradjenta and Janumet.
  • Taking one of these? Check how your plan covers it in 2027.

Under 65?

  • Well-woman visits, mammograms and birth control are still covered with no out-of-pocket cost on most private plans. The Supreme Court upheld this in June 2025.
  • Women 50 to 64 not yet on Medicare, with incomes above 400% of the poverty level, lose all help with Marketplace premiums in 2027. Compare plans carefully.

Helping a loved one? Bring her medication list and doctors’ names, and call SHIP for free one-on-one help.
